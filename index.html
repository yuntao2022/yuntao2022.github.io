About Me

I am a PhD student in Applied Mathematics at the University of California, Berkeley. My research interests lie at the intersection of stochastic processes and computational physics.

I study enhanced sampling methods for molecular systems, with a particular interest in the mathematical foundations of metadynamics. I am interested in understanding when these methods converge, how quickly they converge, and how theory can guide their practical use.

My background includes molecular dynamics simulations of biomolecules using empirical and machine-learning potentials.

Research Interests

  • Stochastic Processes and Enhanced Sampling: Langevin dynamics, metadynamics, convergence analysis, and sampling of metastable systems.
  • Computational Biophysics: Protein dynamics, molecular simulation, and the exploration of free-energy landscapes.
  • Learning Collective Variables: Low-dimensional representations of molecular configurations and their role in sampling complex systems.

Talks

An Introduction to Metadynamics: Adiabatic Assumption, Asymptotic Behavior, and Convergence

An introduction to well-tempered metadynamics, its asymptotic integro-differential equation, and convergence results for special forms of biasing hills.

Abstract

Metadynamics is a widely used enhanced sampling method in biophysics for exploring the free-energy landscapes of complex molecular systems. This talk presents a mathematical formulation of well-tempered metadynamics, derives an integro-differential equation governing its asymptotic behavior, and discusses convergence results for three special forms of biasing hills.
